## Preface

The **Planck-Vortex Theory (PVT): V07 (EN)** is developed within the framework of the **Monistic Continuum Model (MCM)** and investigates the emergence of matter from a single ontological foundation.

The central premise of the theory is that physical reality does not consist of fundamentally independent entities such as particles, fields, or spacetime structures. Instead, all observable phenomena arise through the organization of a single continuum and its internal dynamics.

Within this framework, light is interpreted as a freely propagating wave of the continuum. Matter emerges when wave dynamics form stable vortex structures. Electrons, quark rings, baryonic geometries, charge states, and spinality are therefore not regarded as fundamental objects, but as successive manifestations of geometric organization within the same underlying medium.

The objective of this work is to construct a coherent emergence hierarchy capable of connecting continuum dynamics to observable matter through a minimal set of ontological assumptions. Particular emphasis is placed on conceptual consistency, geometric interpretation, and explicit falsifiability.

The theory develops progressively from the continuum and its tension field through dynamics, waves, vortices, residual vortices, electrons, quark rings, and baryonic structures. Charge quantization and baryonic charge states are interpreted as consequences of the internal organization of these geometric configurations.

The present version should be understood as a geometric and ontological framework rather than a complete quantitative theory. While the mathematical foundations of the emergence hierarchy are formulated, the derivation of measurable quantities such as particle masses and coupling constants remains the subject of future work.

The central hypothesis of the **Primary Vortex Theory** may be summarized in a single statement:

> **All observable matter structures emerge from the organization of a single continuum through stable vortex dynamics.**

This work represents an exploration of that hypothesis and its consequences for the emergence of electrons, quark structures, charge quantization, and baryonic matter.
